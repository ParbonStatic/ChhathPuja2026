01 // THE SACRED LORE

Why Chhath stands apart

No idols, no priests, no walls — only a devotee, the river, and the sun.

✎ equality, but make it a river

Setting & Rising Sun

Chhath is among the rare festivals that honours both the Sandhya Arghya to the setting sun and the Usha Arghya to the rising sun — revering life's every cycle, the fall and the rise alike, without discrimination.

Pavitrata & Nature

Supreme cleanliness governs every step. The festival binds people to rivers, ponds and the soil; at the Ganga ghats rich and poor, every caste and community, stand shoulder to shoulder in the same water.

Chhathi Maiya

Devotees venerate Chhathi Maiya (Shashthi Devi), sister of Surya in popular belief, as protector of children — praying for their well-being, long life and the prosperity of the entire family.

“उगा हो सूरज देव, भोर भिनसार...”“Rise, O Surya Dev, in the early dawn” — a refrain heard at every ghat.
02 // THE 36-HOUR GRIND

Chaar Din Ka Mahaparv

Select a day to follow the vrati's 36-hour journey of discipline and devotion.

tap a day →
Day 1

Nahay-Khay

नहाय-खाय

The vrati takes a purifying bath in the river (Ganga jal is brought home where the river is far) and cleans the house thoroughly. Only then is the satvik meal cooked and eaten.

  • Kaddu-Bhat – bottle gourd (lauki) curry with rice
  • Chana Dal – cooked in ghee with rock salt
  • Prepared in brass or clay utensils, free of onion and garlic
Day 2

Kharna / Lohanda

खरना / लोहंडा

A full day of nirjala fasting — not even a sip of water — broken only after sunset with prayers to the earth and Chhathi Maiya. Family and neighbours then receive the prasad with folded hands.

  • Gur ki Kheer (Rasiya) of rice, milk and jaggery
  • Roti and ripe bananas
  • Cooked on a mitti ka chulha fuelled by mango wood
  • The 36-hour waterless fast begins right after the meal
Day 3

Sandhya Arghya (Pehla Arghya)

संध्या अर्घ्य

The whole family walks to the riverbank in procession, the bamboo Daura carried on the head and the Soop filled with fruit and thekua, singing geet all the way.

  • Standing waist-deep, the vrati offers raw milk and water to the setting sun
  • Soops are held up as the family pours arghya over them
  • The Koshi — earthen diyas under sugarcane canopy — is lit at home or the ghat
Day 4

Usha Arghya (Bhorva Arghya) & Paran

उषा अर्घ्य एवं पारण

Long before dawn the devotees are back in the water, lamps floating around them, awaiting the first ray. The final arghya is offered to the rising sun.

  • Prasad is shared with everyone present at the ghat
  • The 36-hour nirjala fast is broken (Paran) with prasad and water
  • The vrati seeks the blessings of elders at home
03 // MAHAPRASAD

Thekua, Soop & the Harvest

Everything is homemade, kept pure, and offered first to the Sun.

✎ sanchas carved by hand, always

Thekua / Khajuria

ठेकुआ

The signature prasad of wheat flour, jaggery, ghee and cardamom, deep-fried and pressed on carved wooden saanchas to leave floral and wheel patterns.

AataGurGheeElaichi

Kasar Laddoo

कसार / चावल के लड्डू

Coarse roasted rice powder bound with melted jaggery and ghee into soft, fragrant laddoos — a humble, sweet offering to Chhathi Maiya.

Rice flourJaggeryGhee

Fruits of the Season

फल एवं सूप सामग्री

Every soop is heaped with the harvest: giant pomelo (Daabh Nimbu), sugarcane with green shoots (Ikh), fresh ginger, turmeric pods, and water chestnuts (Singhara).

Daabh NimbuIkhAdrakHaldiSinghara

Bamboo Craft

सूप, दौरा और कोनिया

The woven Soop, Daura and Konian are hand-made by rural artisans, mostly from bamboo, and bought fresh each year — a livelihood the festival sustains.

04 // BIHARI FOLK GEET

The Voice of the Ghat

Chhath is unthinkable without its songs, made immortal by voices such as the late Padma Bhushan Sharda Sinha, “Bihar Kokila”.

✎ play these in your head, now
केलवा के पात पर उगेलन सूरजमल
झाँके-झुके...

“On the banana leaf, Suraj Dev has risen — peeping, bending to look at the devotees.” The sun is portrayed as a loving guest at the ghat.

Folk tradition · Sharda Sinha
उ जे केरवा जे फरेला घवद से,
ओह पर सुगा मँडराय...

“The banana hangs in a heavy bunch, and a parrot hovers over it.” A parrot tries to peck the offering — a tender folk image of guarding prasad's purity; the parrot is warned away and the vrati prays on.

Folk tradition · Sharda Sinha
